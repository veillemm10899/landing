01 — The Atelier

EST. MMXIX
Our story

Built slowly,
on purpose.

VEILLEMM began in a spare room in Manila with a loupe, a lathe, and a stubborn idea: that a wristwatch should be an heirloom, not an accessory. Founder Cyrus Vergara spent three years prototyping before the first numbered piece ever left the bench.

Today the atelier still works the same way — one watchmaker per watch, no assembly line, no corners. If we wouldn't pass a piece down to our own children, it doesn't leave the room.

  • 01

    One watchmaker, one watch — signed on the caseback.

  • 02

    Never more than 100 a year — scarcity by discipline, not marketing.

  • 03

    Lifetime service — every watch returns to the bench that made it.

“ A watch is the only machine you wear on your body every day.
It should be worth the wrist. — Cyrus Vergara, Founder

03 — Craftsmanship

Details you
will notice.

The difference between a watch and a timepiece lives in what you can't see from a distance. These six disciplines are non-negotiable on every piece we make.

01

In-house Calibre CO-01

28,800 vibrations per hour, 72-hour reserve, designed and assembled under one roof.

02

Hand-finished movement

Geneva stripes, bevelled bridges and polished screw heads — visible through the caseback.

03

Domed sapphire

Anti-reflective on both faces. Scratched sapphire is a problem we have never met.

04

Steel & gold, properly

316L surgical steel and 18k gold, brushed and polished by hand to catch light at the right angle.

05

Regulated in six positions

Fifteen days on the timing machine before any watch is allowed to meet its wrist.

06

Lifetime care

Five-year warranty and free servicing for life. A VEILLEMM is bought once, kept forever.

04 — The Making

From sketch
to wrist.

Twelve weeks, five hands, one signature. This is the journey of every numbered VEILLEMM — from the first pencil line to the caseback engraving of its owner's name.

  1. 01

    Design & sketch

    Proportions, legibility and balance are argued over paper for weeks before a single component is cut.

  2. 02

    Machining & finissage

    Cases, bridges and wheels are cut, then decorated by hand — stripes, bevels, mirror polish.

  3. 03

    Assembly

    One watchmaker builds the entire movement and case from start to finish, signing the caseback.

  4. 04

    Regulation

    Fifteen days, six positions, three temperatures. Timing is corrected until it stops being a question.

  5. 05

    Numbering & delivery

    Individually numbered, engraved to order, and delivered in a case your grandchildren will fight over.
